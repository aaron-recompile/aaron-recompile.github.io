Is CSFS+CAT already a general covenant primitive?

Capability Parity of CSFS+CAT with CTV and ANYPREVOUT for Common Covenant Patterns: On-Chain Evidence from Inquisition Signet

With confirmed transactions on Bitcoin Inquisition signet, OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK plus OP_CAT reproduces the covenant capabilities of CTV and ANYPREVOUT for output binding, input binding and eltoo-style state replacement, for signer-participating spends.

Aaron Zhang29 pages✓ 3 on-chain demonstrations
Read the PDFSSRNDelving Bitcoin

Abstract

We show, with confirmed on-chain transactions on Bitcoin Inquisition signet, that the combination of OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK (BIP 348) and OP_CAT (BIP 347) reproduces the covenant capabilities of OP_CHECKTEMPLATEVERIFY (BIP 119) and BIP 118 ANYPREVOUT for three common patterns: output binding and input binding, built directly from CSFS+CAT, and the eltoo state-replacement pattern, realized through a CSFS delegation ladder with CTV settlement. The claim covers spends where the spending signer participates.

The approach rests on two techniques. Same-signature binding: one Schnorr signature must satisfy both OP_CHECKSIG against the node-computed sighash and OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK against a witness-provided preimage, which forces the witness preimage to be byte-identical to the real one. Preimage decomposition: OP_CAT, OP_SIZE and witness padding emulate the disabled OP_SUBSTR, so Script can extract and verify any field of the preimage. Together they recover sha_outputs, sha_prevouts and sha_amounts.

What the paper does not claim

  • Capability, not efficiency: these scripts and witnesses are larger, and the dedicated opcodes are far cheaper.
  • Signer-participating spends only; keyless covenants are out of scope.
  • Not an argument that CAT is harmless, and no position for or against activating any opcode.

Reproduce

Every construction can be checked from its confirmed witness on a block explorer; no private keys are needed. The reproduction package lives in btcaaron.
